# Attractor-family mechanistic branch: AR2–AR6

This record is scientifically separate from the current ALI claim. It preserves the later attractor-family trajectory experiments, including the negative causal boundary reached in AR6. It should not be read as evidence that trajectories create information or that dynamics are uniquely necessary.

## AR2 — endpoint-controlled route structure

Within frozen families of locally stable input-conditioned equilibria, pre-convergence approach geometry retained fine-condition-specific structure. Ordered directional history decoded fine condition better than the best single transient and far better than shuffled history, including among tightly endpoint-matched examples. A matched static nonlinear readout from `z` was at least as strong, so dynamics were not uniquely necessary.

## AR3 — positional/progress binding

The native-vs-shuffle effect reproduced, but fixed reversal and coarse fixed permutations preserved native-level performance. A progress-keyed permutation-invariant observer recovered essentially the native result. Cross-time decoder transfer was strongly local and progress itself was decodable from individual directions. The strongest irreducible-forward-chronology interpretation was therefore rejected in favor of progress-conditioned reformatting.

## AR4 — affine reformatting and predictive Jacobian geometry

Distant-stage raw transfer was near chance, while orthogonal alignment recovered a large fraction and general affine alignment recovered about 92.5% of the lost far-stage accessibility. Middle-stage canonicalization restored broad cross-time compatibility. The family of fitted stage transforms was low-dimensional and approximately compositional. Accumulated evolving Jacobians predicted later directional orientation substantially better than a frozen starting Jacobian.

AR4 therefore supported an observational/predictive mechanism: persistent fine-condition-associated structure is expressed through changing, approximately affine stage coordinates associated with evolving local dynamical geometry. It did not establish that changing Jacobian geometry causes the native affine reformatting.

## AR5 — local causal transport, insufficient geometry change

Small intermediate-state interventions were propagated almost perfectly by Jacobian linearization. However, the admissible state perturbation changed the realized Jacobian path by only about 0.036% in relative Frobenius norm. Evolving, frozen, shuffled, and global Jacobian transport were consequently almost indistinguishable. AR5 established a local causal transport law but could not test meaningful changed-geometry causality.

## AR6 — operator-level control and causal boundary

AR6 directly modified the local update operator for a temporary two-step window rather than merely moving the state. Validation selected the middle stage and `alpha=0.10` independently in all five frozen systems.

The intervention changed local Jacobian geometry by about **10.8%**, roughly three hundred times AR5's realized change, while preserving coarse output identity in **100%** of tested examples, passing the endpoint tolerance in **100%**, and producing mean terminal displacement of only about **6.20e-8**.

The operator also produced highly controlled signed transient coordinate motion: `+K` versus `-K` had mean sign-reversal cosine about **0.9963**.

But the preregistered AR4-derived format prediction failed. The supposed format-advance intervention reduced later-stage reader compatibility in all five systems, mean **-3.50 pp**, and a matched random operator produced a nearly identical mean reduction (**-3.36 pp**). The retard intervention strongly reduced later-reader compatibility but did not reproducibly improve earlier-reader compatibility.

Modified-operator Jacobians predicted the short counterfactual accurately (~0.99165 cosine), but native-path Jacobians were essentially as accurate (~0.99158); the advantage was tiny and heterogeneous. Canonicalized representation also moved almost as much as raw representation, so AR6 did not establish a clean causal format/content separation.

**AR6 primary classification: Outcome C — operator geometry can be causally manipulated, but the specific AR4 transform family did not earn causal privilege.**

Narrow conclusion:

> Direct modification of the local update operator can strongly change local dynamical geometry and causally produce signed changes in transient representational coordinates while leaving coarse output and terminal state essentially unchanged. However, those changes do not follow the preregistered AR4 format-advance prediction, are not privileged over matched random operator changes on the key reader test, and do not preserve the canonical representation. AR4's affine/Jacobian model therefore remains a strong description of native progress-conditioned reformatting, but AR6 does not establish it as the mesoscopic causal law generating that reformatting.

## Branch decision

AR6 closes this increasingly fine mechanistic escalation. Per the locked decision rule, there should be no AR7 designed merely to force stronger causality. The appropriate next direction is architecture generalization while carrying forward both the positive observations and the causal boundaries.
