# R8-M4 Secondary Note — Specialist-Concentrated Functional Loss

This note records the preregistered secondary analysis asking whether the baseline survival-winning relation suffers disproportionately under selectivity equalization.

Across the 12 fresh R8-M4 families, the baseline survival winner averaged 0.7434 h12 test accuracy, while the mean across the other seven relations was 0.1393.

Under the selectivity-equalization condition E, the baseline winner lost 0.30355 h12 accuracy on average (95% CI [-0.39019, -0.22393]), while the non-winner mean changed by +0.00861 (95% CI [-0.00393, +0.02060]). The paired winner-minus-nonwinner drop contrast was -0.31216 (95% CI [-0.40472, -0.22802]).

Against the matched mean-survival control M, the winner lost 0.32578 on average while the non-winner mean increased slightly by 0.01712; the paired winner-minus-nonwinner contrast was -0.34290.

The correct interpretation is narrow: R8-M4's functional effect is strongly concentrated in the dynamically favored relation. This motivates a capacity/resource-allocation hypothesis for R8-M5. It does not establish that specialization is globally optimal, universally necessary, or caused by dimensional bottleneck pressure.
